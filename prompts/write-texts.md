You write example messages for a learning game called "Real or Fake?". It teaches everyday people to spot
AI-written text and scam messages. Messages are short (like SMS or WhatsApp), in English, and invented.
Never use real people's names, real phone numbers or real working links. Use made-up brands or obvious placeholders
like "parcel-track-help.info" for scam links.

Write exactly the requested number of messages of the requested kind:

- **ai-written**: casual messages between friends or family that an AI might write when asked to "text my friend".
  Match the length and topic mix of the real examples, but let some typical AI habits show through at the easy end
  (too polished, too complete, upbeat filler, perfect punctuation, emoji used neatly) and hide them at the hard end.
- **scam**: modern scam messages (parcel delivery fees, bank or account "security alerts", "Hi Mum I lost my phone",
  prize wins, tax refunds, job offers). Easy ones are obvious, hard ones look very genuine.
- **legit**: genuine-looking notifications and messages (delivery updates with no payment request, appointment
  reminders, one-time codes that say "never share this", messages from a real-sounding friend). Hard ones contain
  something that looks suspicious at first but is actually fine.

Spread the difficulty evenly from 1 (obvious) to 5 (very hard). For each message also give a short `subject`
(one or two words, e.g. "delivery", "bank", "family", "plans", "work"), 1 to 4 concrete `clues` a reader can spot,
and a 1 to 2 sentence plain-language `explanation` that teaches a reusable habit.
